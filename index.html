Key Findings
🏠

Homeownership is out of reach in two of three markets

Bloomington and Winston-Salem production workers face mortgage gaps of -$8.31/hr & -$7.98/hr respectively, meaning the median production wage falls roughly $8/hr short of what's needed to afford a median home under the 30% rule. Canton is the only Cook market where a single production income clears the homeownership threshold.

⚠️

Bloomington is tight, specialized, and about to get a talent injection

An Assembler LQ of 3.97, nearly 4× the national concentration, means Cook already operates in an extremely specialized talent pool. Novo Nordisk's May 2026 layoffs will release ~400 skilled biomanufacturing workers into that same pool. This is a narrow, time-sensitive window.

📈

Winston-Salem is Cook's most scalable long-term market

With 22,950 production workers, +7.4% population growth since 2015, and a stable mobility rate of 11.3%, Winston-Salem has the strongest structural fundamentals for sustained hiring. The competitive challenge here is differentiation, not access. Cook competes in a fragmented employer landscape rather than a scarce labor pool.

📉

Peoria/Canton's shrinking population is a long-horizon risk

Canton's affordability advantage [ the only market with a positive mortgage gap ] is real and should be leveraged in retention messaging. But a population decline of 11.5% since 2015 puts a structural ceiling on the hiring pool over the next decade. Cook's Canton strategy should emphasize deep retention over volume recruiting.

Recommendations
01
Bloomington · Immediate

Activate Novo Nordisk recruitment pipeline

~400 skilled biomanufacturing workers enter the Bloomington labor market in May 2026. Cook should prepare targeted outreach before other local employers act. QA, production, and maintenance profiles are directly transferable to medical device manufacturing.

02
Bloomington · Retention

Address the housing affordability gap with targeted assistance

Bloomington production workers face an -$8.31/hr mortgage shortfall. Ellettsville and Spencer, where Cook also operates, have materially better affordability profiles. Informs where retention investment is most effective.

03
Winston-Salem · Differentiation

Compete on offer quality, not wage escalation alone

Winston-Salem is a fragmented, competition-driven market. Cook's edge will come from hiring speed, employer branding, and visible career pathways. Formalize assembler → QA → supervisor progression and make it a recruiting message.

04
Winston-Salem · Scale

Scale recruiting channels, the pool supports it

With 22,950 production workers and a growing population, Winston-Salem supports broader sourcing strategies than either Bloomington or Peoria. Invest in pipeline volume, not just individual hires. This is Cook's most sustainable long-term hiring market.

05
Canton · Retention

Lead with Canton's affordability advantage in retention messaging

Canton is the only Cook location where production median wage clears the homeownership threshold. This is a differentiating retention message, "work here, own a home here", that no Bloomington or Winston-Salem employer can match at the same wage level.

06
Canton · Long-horizon

Prioritize retention depth over volume recruiting in Canton

An 11.5% population decline since 2015 puts a structural ceiling on Peoria/Canton's future labor supply. Cook's Canton strategy should maximize tenure and internal development rather than plan for high-volume replacement hiring. The pool will not replenish easily.
